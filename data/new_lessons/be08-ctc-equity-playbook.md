# Lesson 08 — India Tech CTC Breakdown & Equity Playbook

> **Priority:** ★★★★★ (Directly protects a large share of your first-year pay during offer negotiations)  
> **Estimated Study Time:** 55 minutes  
> **Key Skills:** Indian CTC Decomposition, Base vs Bonus vs Retirals vs RSUs, Vesting schedules (even vs back-loaded), ESOP Perquisite Tax, Competing offer leverage scripts.

> **A note on numbers.** This lesson deliberately contains no salary or tax figures. Compensation bands, tax rules and vesting schedules change, differ by company and level, and are easy to quote wrongly. Use your own offer letter and a current, dated source (for example a levels data site and a tax professional) for every number you act on.

---

## 1. Anatomy of an Indian Tech Offer

Recruiters often headline one large CTC figure (e.g. *"Congratulations, we are offering you a CTC of X!"*). Break that single number into its constituent buckets before you react to it:

| Component | What it is | What actually reaches you in Year 1 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fixed base salary** | Guaranteed monthly pay | Base minus income tax and your PF contribution | **Your next hike and your bonus target are usually calculated on this alone.** |
| **Annual target bonus** | A percentage of base, paid on company and individual rating | Target is not a promise; payout varies | Ask what the payout has been in the last few cycles. |
| **Joining bonus** | One-time cash payment | Taxed as income | Often carries a clawback if you leave within a set period. Read the clause. |
| **Stock / RSUs (Year 1 portion)** | Grant value spread over the vesting schedule | Only the tranche that has vested is yours; value moves with the share price | The headline often counts the grant at today's price. |
| **Retirals (PF, gratuity)** | Employer contributions | Locked until you leave the company or retire; gratuity needs a minimum service period | Counted inside the headline CTC but not spendable. |

**Exercise with your own offer.** Write each line for the first year and compute:
`Year-1 headline = base + target bonus + joining bonus + Year-1 stock tranche + retirals`
`Year-1 spendable = base + bonus actually paid + joining bonus + vested stock sold, minus tax and PF`
The gap between the two is the number to negotiate around, not the headline.

---

## 2. RSUs (Public Stock) vs ESOPs (Startups)

- **RSUs (large listed companies):** Publicly traded stock. When a tranche vests it can usually be sold on the exchange where it is listed, subject to the company's trading windows and tax rules.
- **ESOPs (pre-IPO startups):** Options to buy shares at a strike price.
  - ⚠️ **The double-tax trap:** you can owe income tax (perquisite tax) on the difference between fair market value and strike price *when you exercise*, even if the company has not listed and the shares cannot be sold yet. Check the current rule, including any deferral available to eligible startup employees, before you exercise.
- **Vesting schedules differ.** Some companies vest evenly each year, others back-load the grant toward later years. The schedule changes how much of the headline you will actually hold if you leave after one or two years, so compare offers on the schedule as well as the total.

## 3. The 3 Word-For-Word Negotiation Scripts

Fill the bracketed values from your own research. Do not quote a number you cannot support.

### Scenario 1: Recruiter asks for current salary on the first phone screen
> *"I'd rather not share my current compensation. More importantly, I'm evaluating opportunities based on the scope of the Senior Engineering role at your company and market data for that level. Based on my research for roles at this level, I am targeting a base of [your target base] with equity. If we find a strong mutual fit in the technical rounds, I'm confident we can agree on a competitive package."*

### Scenario 2: You have an offer and want to counter
> *"Thank you so much for the offer. I am genuinely excited about the team's roadmap. While reviewing the package, I noticed the base salary is below what I anticipated for this scope, especially given my strong feedback in the system design round. I am also in final rounds with another company where the base is around [competing base]. If you can bring the base to [your counter] with a joining bonus of [amount], I would be glad to sign and withdraw from other loops."*

### Scenario 3: You want more time to decide
> *"I'm very interested and want to give this the consideration it deserves. Could you extend the deadline to [date]? I'd also like to understand the vesting schedule, the bonus payout history, and any clawback terms in writing so I can compare the offers fairly."*
